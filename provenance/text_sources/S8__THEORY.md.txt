# Measurement geometry and collective threshold extension

**6 September 2026. Separate research continuation.** These are new deductions from the noisy-record channel study, except for the explicitly identified source lemmas and the reused arithmetic engine. No accepted manuscript, figure, dialogue report, or remote repository is changed. Literature priority has not been established.

## 1. Channel and operational meaning

Choose finitely many unit Bloch axes $n_b\in\mathbb R^3$, probabilities $w_b>0$ with $\sum_b w_b=1$, and independent symmetric sign-error probabilities $0<\epsilon_b<1/2$. A channel use either transmits the input qubit intact, with probability $1-p$, or destructively measures axis $b$, chosen with probability $w_b$, and delivers $(b,s)$ with the outcome bit flipped at probability $\epsilon_b$.

Write $\eta_b=1-2\epsilon_b$ and

$$E_{b,s}=\frac{I+(-1)^s\eta_b n_b\cdot\sigma}{2},$$
$$\mathcal N_p(\rho)=(1-p)\rho\ \oplus\ p\sum_{b,s}w_b\operatorname{Tr}(E_{b,s}\rho)|b,s\rangle\langle b,s|.$$

The flag identifies the surviving or measured branch. True outcomes and discarded qubits are inaccessible. Encoding precedes the random choices. The channel is memoryless; all physical choices and sign flips are independent across uses. There is no feedback, preshared entanglement, outcome postselection, or imperfect quantum gate model. The theorem does not cover arbitrary POVMs, asymmetric reporting errors, corrupted basis labels, or correlated noise.

This is an incomplete-erasure channel in the established identity-direct-sum-auxiliary-channel construction [R1]. The present question concerns its optimized single-use coherent information versus achievable quantum capacity:

$$Q^{(1)}(\mathcal N_p)=\max_\rho I_c(\rho,\mathcal N_p),\qquad
Q(\mathcal N_p)\geq \frac1n I_c(\rho_n,\mathcal N_p^{\otimes n}).$$

The second inequality has the usual asymptotic coding meaning [R2]. A positive finite inner-block quantity implies an asymptotic rate after outer coding. It does not claim high-fidelity recovery by the inner block alone or an efficient decoder.

## 2. Exact optimized one-use benchmark, defined without a weak-input shortcut

For a unit direction $u$, write

$$\rho_{t,u}=\tfrac12[I+(1-2t)u\cdot\sigma],\qquad 0\leq t\leq\tfrac12.$$

Let $C(t,u)$ be the conditional-reference entropy averaged over the measured branch. It is not the entropy of the classical record. The exact expression is

$$C(t,u)=\sum_{b,s}w_b F(q_{b,s},d_b),$$
$$q_{b,s}=\frac{1+(-1)^s\eta_b(1-2t)n_b\cdot u}{2},\qquad
d_b=\epsilon_b(1-\epsilon_b)t(1-t),$$
$$F(q,d)=q h_2\left(\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4d/q^2}}2\right).$$

Thus

$$I_c(t,u)=(1-p)h_2(t)-pC(t,u).$$

Define the genuinely global entropy-cost ratio

$$\Gamma=\inf_{u,\ 0<t\leq1/2}\frac{C(t,u)}{h_2(t)}.$$

This infimum includes all complex qubit inputs and arbitrarily nearly pure inputs. Since pure inputs have coherent information zero,

$$\boxed{Q^{(1)}(\mathcal N_p)=0\iff p\geq\frac1{1+\Gamma}.}$$

Below that value, the definition of the infimum supplies a positive input. At or above it every mixed input is nonpositive. This variational characterization is exact but is not, on its own, a solved scalar or angular optimization problem. Later sections supply useful globally certified lower bounds on $\Gamma$.

The near-pure directional ratio, useful but not a substitute for $\Gamma$, is

$$g(u)=\lim_{t\downarrow0}\frac{C(t,u)}{h_2(t)}
=\sum_b w_b\frac{1-\eta_b^2}{1-\eta_b^2(n_b\cdot u)^2}.$$

In particular $\Gamma\leq\min_u g(u)$. Replacing this inequality by equality without proof would give a potentially invalid zero-single-use claim.

## 3. Two record coefficients determine long balanced repetitions

Choose the antipodal pure states $|u_+\rangle,|u_-\rangle$ and the balanced repetition input

$$\rho_n=\tfrac12|u_+\rangle\langle u_+|^{\otimes n}
+\tfrac12|u_-\rangle\langle u_-|^{\otimes n}.$$

Define

$$s_b(u)=\sqrt{1-\eta_b^2(n_b\cdot u)^2},$$
$$\kappa_b(u)=\frac{\eta_b^2[1-(n_b\cdot u)^2]}{1-\eta_b^2(n_b\cdot u)^2},$$
$$B(u)=\sum_b w_b s_b(u),\qquad K(u)=\sum_b w_b s_b(u)\kappa_b(u).$$

The difference simplifies to

$$\boxed{\Lambda(u)=B(u)-K(u)=\sum_b w_b\frac{1-\eta_b^2}{\sqrt{1-\eta_b^2(n_b\cdot u)^2}}.}$$

$B$ is the classical Bhattacharyya overlap between the reported-record distributions generated by the two pure codewords. Opposite outcomes exchange those distributions, so their classical Chernoff minimum occurs at exponent $1/2$. $K$ weights the same record overlap by the retained normalized squared coherence. These coefficients are derived from the physical noisy record, not from revealing the hidden true outcome. Record-overlap and discrimination exponents are established tools [R3]; their use in the following signed coherent-information comparison is the construction developed here.

### 3.1 Exact record sum

For one record symbol $y=(b,s)$ define

$$P_\pm(y)=w_b\frac{1\pm(-1)^s\eta_b(n_b\cdot u)}2.$$

For a sequence of $m$ measured records let $a=\prod_jP_+(y_j)$, $b=\prod_jP_-(y_j)$, $q=(a+b)/2$, $x=b/(a+b)$, and $y=\prod_j\kappa_{b_j}$. The conditional two-dimensional reference/output matrix has diagonal $(1-x,x)$ and off-diagonal magnitude $\sqrt{y x(1-x)}$. Phases depend on the axes but do not alter its eigenvalues. Define

$$D_y(x)=h_2(x)-h_2\left(\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4(1-y)x(1-x)}}2\right),$$
$$A_m=\sum_{\text{records}}q h_2(x),\qquad
R_m=\sum_{\text{records}}qD_y(x),\qquad C_m=A_m-R_m.$$

Set $A_0=R_0=1$, $C_0=0$. If at least one qubit survives, the conditional receiver entropy supplies $A_m$ and the joint entropy supplies $C_m$. If all qubits are measured the receiver has no remaining quantum entropy, and the contribution is $-C_n$. Therefore

$$\boxed{I_n=\sum_{m=0}^n {n\choose m}(1-p)^{n-m}p^m R_m-p^n A_n.}$$

The $m=n$ terms combine to $-p^nC_n$. This expression sums all flags and all unfavorable branches. Nothing is postselected.

### 3.2 General polynomial envelopes: proof for unequal axes and errors

Let $J$ be the finite number of axes. Reorient the signs of axes if necessary so that $a_b=(1+|\eta_b n_b\cdot u|)/2\geq b_b=1-a_b$. Both are strictly positive. Set

$$L=\sum_b\log(a_b/b_b),\quad x_*=(1+e^L)^{-1},$$
$$c_A=h_2(x_*)>0,\qquad c_R=\frac2{\ln2}x_*(1-x_*)>0.$$

Then, for every integer $m\geq0$,

$$\frac{c_A}{(m+1)^J}B^m\leq A_m\leq B^m,$$
$$\frac{c_R}{(m+1)^J}K^m\leq R_m\leq K^m.$$

For the upper bounds use the inherited entropy inequalities

$$h_2(x)\leq2\sqrt{x(1-x)},\qquad
\frac2{\ln2}y x(1-x)\leq D_y(x)\leq yh_2(x).$$

The first record sum becomes the product of single-record overlaps; the second includes the multiplicative $\kappa$ factors. This gives $B^m$ and $K^m$.

For the lower bounds condition on the count $n_b$ of each measurement axis. For each axis retain only a central binomial sign class, with counts differing by zero or one. Its sign multiplicity is at least $2^{n_b}/(n_b+1)$. The total record log-likelihood ratio then has magnitude at most $L$, regardless of $m$. Consequently $h_2(x)\geq c_A$ and $(2/\ln2)x(1-x)\geq c_R$ on that class. Since $q\geq\sqrt{ab}$, the surviving term is at least

$$c_A\prod_b\frac{s_b^{n_b}}{n_b+1}$$

for $A_m$, and the same expression with $c_R$ and $(s_b\kappa_b)^{n_b}$ for $R_m$. Including the multinomial axis weights and using $\prod_b(n_b+1)\leq(m+1)^J$ gives the stated envelopes. Axes with $\kappa_b=0$ contribute zero to the latter bound whenever selected; the formulas remain valid. If $K=0$, $R_m=0$ for $m>0$ and the surviving no-measurement term is treated normally.

The proof requires finitely many axes and full-rank noisy effects. It is not asserted unchanged at zero error or for a continuum of basis labels. The constants are not universal in the number or geometry of the axes; they may be small.

### 3.3 Eventual sign theorem and its correct scope

Put $U=1-p+pK$ and $V=pB$. Summing the envelopes gives

$$\boxed{\frac{c_R}{(n+1)^J}U^n-V^n\leq I_n\leq U^n-\frac{c_A}{(n+1)^J}V^n.}$$

For fixed channel and encoding direction, $U>V$ implies strictly positive coherent information at all sufficiently large finite block lengths. $U<V$ implies negativity at all sufficiently large lengths. Equality is not decided. Negative eventual sign does not exclude an exceptional smaller block above this frontier. Choosing one positive finite block and applying the coding theorem is legitimate even if its rate is very small.

Thus the strict long-balanced-repetition frontier is

$$\boxed{p_{\mathrm{rep}}(u)=\frac1{1+\Lambda(u)}.}$$

Optimizing its direction minimizes $\Lambda$. This generalizes the preceding common-likelihood Pauli calculation to any finite antipodal noisy-axis ensemble, including unequal weights, nonorthogonal axes, and basis-dependent errors.

## 4. A criterion that compares with the actual global one-use benchmark

A genuine interval with $Q^{(1)}=0<Q$ is certified whenever

$$\boxed{\min_u\Lambda(u)<\Gamma.}$$

Specifically, for any direction satisfying the strict inequality,

$$\frac1{1+\Gamma}\leq p<\frac1{1+\Lambda(u)}$$

is such an interval. This is an exact comparison criterion for the sufficient long-block construction, not a necessary condition for arbitrary collective codes or all possible finite-block gains. It remains useful when $\Gamma$ is replaced by a proved global lower bound.

The easier inequality $\Lambda(u)<g(u)$ is not enough. Since $0<s_b\leq1$, it often holds for oblique directions even in a known-basis channel. The global one-use optimizer can choose another direction and invalidate the purported advantage. The following controls and frame bounds make that issue explicit rather than concealing it in a numerical optimization.

## 5. Coplanar axes: incompatibility alone does not give this threshold gain

Suppose all measurement axes lie in a common plane. Choose $u$ perpendicular to it. For this direction all record distributions are identical under the two logical codewords: $B=1$, $\kappa_b=\eta_b^2$. Put

$$\bar c=\sum_b w_b(1-\eta_b^2).$$

For a single axis, the paired entropy cost is increasing with its squared longitudinal Bloch component. Its perpendicular value is bounded below by $(1-\eta_b^2)h_2(t)$, by the entropy inequality in Section 3. Summing gives $C(t,u')\geq\bar c h_2(t)$ for **every** direction $u'$. At the common perpendicular direction the near-pure ratio tends to $\bar c$. Hence

$$\Gamma=\bar c,\qquad p_1=\frac1{1+\bar c}.$$

Also $s_b\leq1$ gives $\Lambda(u')\geq\bar c$ for every direction, with equality at the perpendicular one. Therefore

$$\min_u\Lambda(u)=\Gamma=\bar c.$$

The optimized strict asymptotic balanced-repetition frontier does not extend the global one-use boundary. This includes a random choice of X and Z: those observables do not commute, but their axes have a common perpendicular direction Y.

There is a stronger all-length statement for the perpendicular repetition family with **any** input weight $t$. Here $A_m=h_2(t)$, and the entropy-coherence bound gives $R_m\leq(\sum_b w_b\eta_b^2)^m h_2(t)$. Thus

$$I_n\leq h_2(t)\left[(1-p+p\sum_b w_b\eta_b^2)^n-p^n\right].$$

It is nonpositive at and above $p_1$ for every block length. This does **not** prove that no other code improves a coplanar channel's threshold; nor does it exclude exceptional finite balanced blocks in a different direction. It rules out the claimed strict asymptotic gain from optimizing the particular family used here.

For common error $\epsilon=.01$, $\bar c=.0396$ and $p_1=1/1.0396$. A known Z channel forced to an oblique coding direction with squared projection 1/3 reproduces the Pauli construction's directional gap, but its global one-use boundary is higher. That is a concrete warning against claiming a threshold advantage from a fixed-input comparison.

## 6. A geometry-only sufficient condition for a broad class

Now assume common error $\epsilon$, and define the measurement frame matrix

$$T=\sum_b w_b n_b n_b^{\mathsf T},\qquad\operatorname{Tr}T=1.$$

This second moment does not completely determine the channel or its capacity. It supplies a uniform bound.

### 6.1 Frame lower bound on the globally optimized one-use cost

For fixed $t$, write $\alpha=\epsilon(1-\epsilon)$, $d=\alpha t(1-t)$, and pair the signs in $F$. The inherited integral representation is

$$F(q,d)=\frac1{\ln2}\int_0^\infty\ln\left(1+\frac d{v(v+q)}\right)dv.$$

The paired integrand at squared longitudinal variable $z$ is

$$\ln\frac{(v+1/2+d/v)^2-z/4}{(v+1/2)^2-z/4}.$$

It is increasing and convex in $z$ on the physical domain. Jensen's inequality and $u^{\mathsf T}Tu\geq\lambda$ imply

$$T\succeq\lambda I\quad\Longrightarrow\quad C(t,u)\geq C_{\epsilon,\lambda}(t),$$

where the surrogate cost has paired probabilities

$$q_\pm=\frac{1\pm\eta(1-2t)\sqrt\lambda}{2}$$

and the same determinant $d$. The surrogate is itself a physical single-axis entropy cost at a fixed angle; therefore it is concave in $t$. No assumption of coherent-information concavity is used.

At **exactly** $\epsilon=1/100$, this pass certifies, separately for $\lambda=1/3$ and $\lambda=3/10$,

$$C_{\epsilon,\lambda}(t)\geq g_\lambda h_2(t),\quad
 g_\lambda=\frac{4\epsilon(1-\epsilon)}{1-(1-2\epsilon)^2\lambda}.$$

Each certificate covers arbitrarily nearly pure inputs analytically and [10^-6,1/2] with fourteen exact-rational intervals. The arithmetic is recomputed using Decimal/libmpdec and mpmath.iv. The first case reproduces and repurposes the earlier scalar Pauli certificate; the second is a new parameter certificate. The isotropic and well-conditioned thresholds are respectively

$$p_0^{\rm iso}=\frac{5099}{5396},\qquad
p_0^{.30}=\frac{17797}{18787}.$$

These are **guaranteed zero-from bounds** for the corresponding families. They are not necessarily the exact one-use positivity threshold of each particular non-Pauli channel. The general formula is not claimed numerically certified at every error probability or frame eigenvalue.

### 6.2 Analytic orientation average bounds the repetition loss

For common error put $c=4\epsilon(1-\epsilon)$ and $\eta=1-2\epsilon$. Averaging over all unit coding directions gives

$$\mathbb E_u\Lambda(u)=c\int_0^1\frac{dx}{\sqrt{1-\eta^2 x^2}}
=c\frac{\arcsin\eta}{\eta}.$$

This identity is independent of the measurement-axis ensemble. It is used only to prove the existence of a fixed coding direction: the transmitter does not randomly change its direction after learning the measurement basis. Since $\Lambda$ is continuous on the compact sphere, an attaining minimizing direction exists. Thus

$$\min_u\Lambda(u)\leq c\frac{\arcsin\eta}{\eta},\qquad
p_{\mathrm{rep}}^{\rm guaranteed}=\frac1{1+c\arcsin(\eta)/\eta}.$$

At common error .01 this boundary is

$$0.9475279565620702601602259143462313\ldots.$$

A positive Taylor series with a rigorous tail bounds the arcsine expression; this is not numerical quadrature or a sampled orientation minimum.

### 6.3 Class-wide separation

For **every finite isotropic measurement frame** $T=I/3$, at common error .01,

$$\boxed{0.94495922905856\ldots\leq p<0.94752795656207\ldots
\ \Longrightarrow\ Q^{(1)}=0<Q.}$$

No cubic covariance or equal-projection direction is assumed. Pauli axes and tetrahedral axes allow stronger special-case boundaries; the class-wide bound is deliberately uniform and weaker. Other isotropic frames need not have the same single-use or repetition optimum.

The assertion is not restricted to an exact isotropy manifold. For **every finite frame** with $\lambda_{\min}(T)\geq.30$,

$$\boxed{0.94730398679938\ldots\leq p<0.94752795656207\ldots
\ \Longrightarrow\ Q^{(1)}=0<Q.}$$

The lower endpoint is $17797/18787$. This is a non-fine-tuned geometry condition, allowing perturbations of axes and probabilities. It is sufficient, not necessary. Frames with a smaller minimum eigenvalue are not declared incapable of a gain.

### 6.4 Unequal reporting errors in an explicit robust region

The previous zero bound at the common best error .01 and the positive bound at common worst error .01001 extend by receiver-side degradation to **basis-dependent** errors. Throughout

$$T\succeq.30 I,\qquad .94731\leq p\leq.94740,\qquad
.01\leq\epsilon_b\leq.01001\quad\text{for every }b,$$

one has $Q^{(1)}=0<Q$. At the worst error, the positive boundary is above .94748137608. Both p endpoints are strictly inside the needed analytic inequalities. A receiver can add independent basis-dependent sign flips to pass from smaller errors to the common worst error. All exact masks and basis labels remain accessible.

This is a robust family statement, not a grid of sampled channels. It does not provide a uniform practical block length or a significant lower rate throughout the family; the polynomial constants depend on the finite frame.

## 7. Unequal Pauli probabilities: a finite example and an exact geometric optimizer

For orthogonal Pauli axes with common error, put $x_i=u_i^2$, $a=\eta^2$, and $c=1-a$. Then

$$\Lambda(u)=c\sum_{i=1}^3\frac{w_i}{\sqrt{1-a x_i}},\quad x_i\geq0,\quad\sum_i x_i=1.$$

This is a convex optimization on a simplex. On an active set of positive $x_i$, its KKT equations give

$$1-a x_i=Cw_i^{2/3},\qquad
C=\frac{|A|-a}{\sum_{i\in A}w_i^{2/3}}.$$

Inactive components are zero and satisfy the corresponding derivative inequality. Thus a finite active-set check supplies the globally optimal direction for the **asymptotic repetition exponent**, not for the entire quantum capacity or necessarily for one-use coherent information. Vanishing weights allow a blind direction and give $\min\Lambda=c$.

A simpler fixed-direction example is enough for a certified finite witness. Suppose each Pauli-basis probability is at least .30. Decompose the measured entropy cost into .90 times the equal-Pauli cost and a .10 remainder. Every single-axis cost is at least $c h_2(t)$, so

$$\Gamma\geq .90\frac{297}{5099}+.10\frac{99}{2500}.$$

Hence $Q^{(1)}=0$ for

$$p\geq\frac{127475000}{134662301}=.94662722271469\ldots.$$

Meanwhile the body-diagonal repetition input has the same sign likelihoods and normalized coherence magnitude in every Pauli basis. Its coherent information is independent of the basis probabilities after the labeled records are summed. At $n=32$, $p=.948$, $\epsilon=.01$, this pass encloses

$$\frac{I_{32}}{32}=3.04765413861069806636820808930256\ldots\times10^{-7}>0.$$

It is computed by the previously audited Decimal repetition evaluator and independently re-evaluated from unnormalized effect determinants with mpmath.iv. The same input and bound work for the entire probability simplex region $w_X,w_Y,w_Z\geq.30$, not just $(.4,.3,.3)$. This changes geometry/probability robustness, not the best known Pauli-channel absolute threshold.

For illustration, the KKT formula at weights $(.4,.3,.3)$ gives squared coding components approximately $(.2401285,.3799357,.3799357)$ and the improved family frontier .95431435. These optional optimized values are corroborated numerically, but the finite positive witness uses the simpler body diagonal and does not rely on a floating-point optimizer.

## 8. Interpretation and nonclaims

The criterion separates two competitions. The single-use benchmark minimizes a conditional entropy cost over every input. Long repetitions instead balance record ambiguity against retained conditional coherence. The different statistical weights are visible in $g(u)$, with $s_b^{-2}$, and $\Lambda(u)$, with $s_b^{-1}$. A directional advantage need not beat the globally optimized one-use channel.

Noncommutativity alone is insufficient to explain this particular threshold extension: two noncommuting axes still leave a common blind direction. Well-conditioned full three-dimensional frames give a proved sufficient class at the stated errors. This does not establish that every informationally complete measurement family has the gain, or that full dimension is necessary for arbitrary collective codes.

Nor does the separation mean that spreading measurements over more axes always increases capacity. A coplanar channel can have a higher one-use positivity threshold than the Pauli family's collective sufficient threshold. The comparison $Q^{(1)}=0<Q$ is always **within the same channel**, not a claim of superiority over a different noise process.

The all-code capacity, exact positivity threshold, optimal rate, efficient outer decoder, correlated-noise version, and literature priority remain unresolved. The new result is a structural family extension within an established channel class, not a solved quantum-capacity problem or a statement about a new many-body transition.
